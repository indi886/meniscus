Two pictures in every pane

Every window holds two pictures: the sky it reflects and the room behind it. Dirt sits between them and spoils both. We started on a ladder in Marrickville in 2011 with a fourteen-inch channel and a bucket, and learned the first rule the hard way: if the sun is on the glass, you are not cleaning it, you are printing streaks on it.

So every job is planned around two zeros. Zero parts per million in the water, so nothing is left behind when it dries. Zero sun on the face we are working, so it dries by gravity instead of evaporation. Thirty-eight technicians, eleven vans and two hot-water trailers run that rule from a depot in Alexandria.

Gravity is the schedule

One building, cleaned the way we clean every building: top down, in the shade, signed off with numbers.

  1. 11:02

    Survey

    We walk the site with a sun-path diagram and a laser meter, log every anchor's test tag, and write the shade plan: which face, which hour.

  2. 11:40

    Rig and isolate

    Anchors proof-loaded to 15 kN before anyone hangs from them, two ropes per technician, an exclusion zone taped off below. Nothing starts until the barrier is up.

  3. 12:05

    Purify

    Mains water at 62 ppm runs through carbon, a reverse-osmosis membrane and mixed-bed resin. It leaves the trailer at 000 ppm, measured at the brush, not at the tap.

  4. 12:30

    Roof first

    Top down, always. Gutters vacuumed and flow-tested, then the solar array brushed with pure water. Whatever we loosen up here lands on surfaces we haven't cleaned yet.

  5. 13:20

    Façade

    Soft wash from the parapet down in 2.4-metre drops, rinsed before anything can dry on the face. The south wall is in shade from noon.

  6. 14:10

    Glass

    The sun has gone off the east face. Strip washer, then the fan stroke: one continuous S with the blade at 30°, edges detailed with scrim. The meniscus is the line we follow.

  7. 15:05

    Ground

    Last, because everything above drains onto it. Hot water under a rotary cleaner, every litre vacuum-recovered and taken to sewer under our trade-waste permit.

  8. 16:50

    Reading

    We sign off with numbers instead of a walk-around: parts per million at the brush, light through the glass, kilowatt-hours from the inverter, litres per second from the downpipe.
