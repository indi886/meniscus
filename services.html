Method statements

Every clean on our books follows one of these seven statements. They are the same documents our crews carry, with the safe work method statement, the anchor register and the shade plan attached for your site.

Each ends with the readings we hand you, because a clean you can't measure is just an opinion.

07:00

Office and retail windows

Ground to fourth floor from the footpath, with nothing leaning on your glass and nothing left on it when it dries.

Scope

Ground to fourth-floor glass, frames, sills and mullion faces, worked from the footpath or the car park. Inside faces by arrangement.

Method

  1. Shade check: confirm which elevations are out of direct sun at the booked hour.
  2. Purge and test: the meter at the brush reads 000 ppm before the first pane.
  3. Agitate with a soft brush on a carbon pole, frames first, then glass from the top down.
  4. Rinse with one slow pass of pure water from the head of the frame. No squeegee on pole work: the water dries clean.
  5. Doors and handles squeegeed by hand and finished with scrim.
  6. Light transmission measured through three sample panes per elevation.

Equipment

Carbon water-fed poles to 20 m, a four-stage RO and mixed-bed DI trailer (400 L), 14 to 22-inch channels, microfibre and scrim.

Standards

Work Health and Safety Regulation 2017 (NSW), Part 4.4 Falls. No ladders against glass. An exclusion zone wherever a pole passes over a footpath.

High-rise and multi-storey glass

Two ropes per technician, a face in shade for every drop, and an IRATA Level 3 on every job.

Scope

Curtain wall, spandrel panels and façade fins above the fourth floor, by two-rope access or the building's own BMU.

Method

  1. Anchor audit: every anchor's tag and certificate checked; anything out of date is not used.
  2. Rig working and backup lines on separate anchors, with edge protection at the parapet.
  3. Isolate the base: exclusion zone, and a spotter at every entrance under the drop.
  4. Drop top to bottom in 2.4 m widths: strip washer, squeegee, edges detailed. A drop only runs while its face is in shade.
  5. Re-rig and repeat across the face, west faces before 11:00, east faces after 13:00.
  6. Light transmission sampled from the BMU or the roof edge.

Equipment

IRATA-compliant two-rope systems, descenders with anti-panic, tethered tools in drop bags, radios on every rope.

Standards

AS/NZS 4488 industrial rope access, AS/NZS 1891.4, AS/NZS 5532 for anchors, and the IRATA International Code of Practice.

10:00

Commercial gutters

Proven with a flow test, not with a photograph of a clean gutter.

Scope

Box gutters, eaves gutters, sumps, rainheads and downpipes to the first pit.

Method

  1. Camera survey of each run before anything is touched.
  2. Vacuum with a 3,600 W wet-and-dry gutter vac on 12 m carbon poles, or from the roof on a static line.
  3. Sumps and rainheads cleared by hand.
  4. Downpipes flushed and flow-tested with a measured discharge.
  5. Camera again, before and after in the same frame.

Equipment

Gutter vacuum, carbon poles, a pole-mounted inspection camera, a calibrated bucket and a stopwatch.

13:00

Façades and exterior walls

The gentlest method that works on each material, proven on a test patch first.

Scope

Render, painted surfaces, ACM and fibre-cement cladding, sandstone and face brick.

Method

  1. Test patch: one square metre of each material, assessed after 24 hours.
  2. Render and paint: soft wash below 500 psi with a biodegradable biocide, ten minutes' dwell, rinsed from the top down.
  3. Sandstone and heritage brick: superheated steam at 150 °C and 2 bar, no chemicals, no abrasives.
  4. Cladding: neutral pH and soft brushes; never pressure on panel joints.
  5. Colorimeter readings at marked points, before and after.

Standards

Heritage NSW guidance, and the building's conservation management plan where it has one.

21:00

Pressure cleaning: patios, paths, car parks

Hot water in, every litre back out, and a slip rating when we leave.

Scope

Concrete, pavers and stone, line-marked car parks, loading docks and bin rooms.

Method

  1. Sweep, and pre-treat oil and gum.
  2. Hot water at 85 °C under a 24-inch rotary surface cleaner, in overlapping lanes from the high side down.
  3. Edges and kerbs with a 25° tip.
  4. Recover with a vacuum boom into a 1,000 L tank; discharge to sewer under our Sydney Water trade-waste permit.
  5. Pendulum slip test on the cleaned surface.

Equipment

A 3,000 psi, 21 L/min hot-water unit, rotary surface cleaner, recovery vacuum and pendulum tester.

Standards

AS 4586 slip resistance classification. Nothing reaches the stormwater (Protection of the Environment Operations Act 1997).

05:00

Glass doors, shopfronts and skylights

Finished before seven, so your doors open on glass that isn't there.

Scope

Shopfronts, automatic doors, balustrades, canopies, atria and skylights.

Method

  1. Shopfronts start at 05:00 and finish before seven.
  2. Doors and handles squeegeed, edges scrimmed, tracks vacuumed.
  3. Skylights from a harness on a static line, never from the glazing bars; brushed and rinsed with pure water.
  4. Hard-water etch assessed, then polished with cerium oxide by agreement.
  5. A photograph from the same spot every visit.

Standards

AS/NZS 1891 for horizontal lifelines; AS 1288 decides what we will and won't polish.

05:50

Solar panels

First light, cool glass, pure water, and the inverter as the judge.

Scope

Rooftop and ground-mounted commercial arrays.

Method

  1. Inverter reading the day before.
  2. Start at first light while the glass is cool: cold water on hot glass is a thermal shock.
  3. Soft brush and 000 ppm water under 1.5 bar. No detergent, no pressure.
  4. Walkways and harness lines only. Nobody stands on a panel.
  5. Inverter reading the day after.

Standards

Clean Energy Council guidance and each manufacturer's cleaning terms, so the warranty stands.
